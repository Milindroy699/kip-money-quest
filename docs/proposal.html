Product proposal · November 2026

Kip: Money Quest

A mobile game that teaches Indian teens, aged 13 to 19, to invest from first principles. Each principle is learned by playing it, then tied to the real products they will meet: FDs, SIPs, index funds, bonds, gold, REITs, derivatives and startups. The promise to the player is simple: every lesson makes Future You richer and wiser.

The problem

Teens meet money through tips and FOMO, not principles

Teenagers start earning, saving pocket money and opening bank accounts before anyone explains how money grows or shrinks. School rarely covers investing. Family advice often stops at FDs and gold. The loudest teachers are finfluencers and WhatsApp groups.

Low literacy to start from

The S&P Global FinLit Survey found 24% of Indian adults financially literate. Most teens grow up without a working model of interest, inflation or risk.

Speculation sold as investing

SEBI’s own study found 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lost money (FY22). Young people are a prime audience for “quick profit” content.

The key ideas are invisible

Compounding, inflation and risk play out over years. On a teenager’s time scale they can’t be felt, so they don’t stick.

Finance education doesn’t retain

Text and video courses lose people quickly. Gamified finance apps often reward risk-taking and start to look like betting.

Teens can’t practise for real

Under-18s can’t trade, and shouldn’t. They need a safe place to make mistakes, with no real money and no tips.

Strict rules for minors

India’s DPDP Act requires verifiable parental consent and bars behavioural tracking of children. SEBI rules forbid investment advice.

The idea

Make every principle something you can play, then name the real product

The format blends three things teens already love: a Subway-Surfers-style runner, collectible cards, and Duolingo-style three-minute lessons. A small cast carries the story. At onboarding each player names a dream goal, such as a laptop, college or a bike, and a Future You plan fills in as they learn: the goal’s price after inflation, the monthly SIP that reaches it, the products and the risk mix. Finishing the game produces a Launch Plan they can share with a parent.

Kip the tortoise

The calm, nerdy teacher in big round specs. Wins by consistency, like the fable. His shell grows a ring for every streak week, so compounding shows on the mascot itself.

Dash the hare

Chases hot tips and trades on impulse. Makes the mistakes teens are tempted to make, so the player learns from his, not their own money.

The Melt

A purple fog that stands for inflation. It chases the player in the runner and shrinks any idle coins it touches.

First principles, solved by play

Ten worlds, each built on one principle and one common mistake

Each world takes a principle, names the misconception it fixes, and turns it into a mechanic where the right money behaviour is the winning move. Worlds 6 to 8 are for the 16 to 19 Builder track; younger Explorers go from World 5 straight to World 9.

House rules

The principles every feature follows

  • The mechanic is the lesson

    If you win by doing the right money thing, the lesson lands without a lecture.

  • One honest simulation

    Every mode runs on the same seeded market model, and every projection is labelled hypothetical.

  • Reward reasoning, not luck

    Duels and leagues score reaching the goal, a calm ride and having cash when needed. A reckless all-in can’t win on average.

  • No gambling mechanics

    No loot boxes and no paid currency. Cards are earned by mastery, never bought or drawn.

  • Teach, then ramp up

    Each mechanic starts gentle and gets harder only as the player shows mastery.

  • Value before sign-up

    No account is asked for until the player has finished 3 lessons and a run.

Engagement

The habit loop is itself the lesson

Most apps borrow streaks and rewards from games. Here each one is a money principle in disguise, so coming back every day teaches consistency, compounding and patience.

Key use cases

Who uses it, and for what

Explorer, 13–15

First three minutes to a daily habit

Names a goal, drags a slider to watch Kip’s SIP outgrow Dash’s cash, plays a 45-second Inflation Chase and earns a first card. After that, one 3 to 5 minute lesson, a run and a review a day keep the Knowledge SIP going.

Builder, 16–19

Deeper worlds and real-world detail

Unlocks trading, arbitrage and analysis, plus deep dives on tax, KYC and how products really work. Chart Surfer and Time Tracks join the runner modes.

Competitive player

Leagues and duels that reward good decisions

Weekly leagues across six tiers under pseudonymous handles. Market Duel against Dash or a friend by code: same goal, same market, and the calmer path to the goal wins.

Planner

A real plan for a real goal

The Future You plan builds itself world by world: the goal’s future price, the monthly SIP, where to put it and the risk mix. It ends as a Launch Plan for a first salary.

Parent

Consent, visibility and conversation

Gives consent when a teen under 18 saves progress. Gets a weekly report of what was learned, with dinner-table questions, shared over WhatsApp or email.

School, later

Classroom mode and competitions

Teacher dashboards and an inter-school Investing Olympiad, built on the same simulation. This is a distribution channel as well as a use case.

Safety and compliance

Built for minors from day one

  • Simulated money only: no real trading, no tips and no investment advice.
  • Parental consent for under-18s, with consent records stored, as India’s DPDP Act requires.
  • No behavioural ads or tracking; nudges use only in-app activity, at most one a day, outside quiet hours.
  • No free-text chat. Players appear under pseudonyms and add friends only by code.
  • Every projection is labelled hypothetical, and all numbers go to a SEBI-registered reviewer before launch.

How we’ll know it works

Success measures

  • Activation: 3 lessons, 1 run and a reminder set within 72 hours, plus a return on day 1.
  • Retention: day 1, 7 and 30 return rates, and how long streaks last.
  • Learning: a literacy test before and after, and how well concepts hold up in spaced reviews.
  • Behaviour: the share of duels won with diversified, goal-fit portfolios. This shows the philosophy stuck.

Where it stands

Built and working

  • All 10 worlds and 53 lessons, with an Explorer and a Builder track.
  • Five runner modes, Chart Surfer, Market Duel, and a Practice Portfolio on a shared simulated market.
  • Streaks, freezes, Dividend Day, spaced reviews, nudges and a comeback bonus.
  • Apple and Google sign-in with parental consent, cloud backup and real weekly leagues.
  • An installable Android build; the iOS TestFlight build is next.

What’s next

Before launch, and after

  • Launch: SEBI-registered review of all numbers, a lawyer’s review of consent, privacy policy and terms, device testing, and a 50 to 100 teen beta.
  • Product: friends and Investment Clubs, server-run leagues and automatic parent emails, Dash’s Scam Hotline, a daily Market Minute, Hindi.
  • To decide: the business model. Options include free for teens with school licences, kept free of pay-to-win and of any product sales to minors.

The short version: teens learn money the way they learn games, by doing. Kip turns each investing principle into a game where the right money behaviour is how you win, and the daily habit itself teaches consistency and compounding.
